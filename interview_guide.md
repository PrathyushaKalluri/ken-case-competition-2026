# Interview Guide — Final, Merged, Per-Stakeholder

One ordered question list per stakeholder type, ready to print and take into the room. No piecing together from other files — every base question (Sricharan's, `research/report-source.md` Pages 7-8, verified verbatim) and every additive probe this project has built (`research_plan.md` §3, §6a, §8; `narrowed_problem.md` Decision 1/1b) is merged into one sequence, in the order to actually ask them. Two questions were hardened against the Mom Test during this pass (`research_plan.md` §9) — marked **[HARDENED]** below.

**Before every interview**: explain voluntary participation, ask consent for notes/recording/photography/submission separately, per the script in `research/report-source.md` Page 6. Don't mention an agent or AI until the very end, if at all. Allow 25-35 minutes for households, 20-25 for technicians, plus a 10-minute solo debrief immediately after — every time, not just when convenient.

## A. Household guide — used for stakeholders #1, #2 (Eluru), #5 (My Home), #7 (Standalone Hyderabad), #8 (IIITH campus)

1. **"Tell me about the most recent time you arranged installation, service or repair for a machine at home."** *(Probe: when, which machine, where you lived, your role.)*
2. **"What happened first?"** *(Repeat "And then?" until the story ends — don't impose categories yet.)*
3. **"Could you walk me through the contacts you made?"**
   → **"When this happened, who actually resolved it — did you contact someone yourself, or did it go through a building/society/management system of some kind?"**
   → *If Eluru respondent specifically:* **"Does your building or area have any kind of shared maintenance service, or does everyone arrange their own?"**
4. **"What information did you need at that point?"**
   → **"Is there anything you've personally built to keep track of this — a note on the fridge, a notebook, a folder of documents, a WhatsApp chat you keep with just yourself, a sticker on the machine, anything like that? Could I see it?"** *(Always ask to see and photograph it, with consent — don't accept a description alone.)*
5. **"How did you decide whom to contact?"**
   → **"Do you already get automatic reminders for anything else — like your vehicle's insurance or PUC, or gas cylinder booking? How does that compare to how you're reminded, or not, about this?"**
6. **"What did the other person commit to?"**
   → **"Is this a formal contract/AMC, or more of an ongoing relationship with someone specific you call?"**
7. **"Tell me about any point where the next action was unclear."**
8. **"How was the cost decided?"** *(Probe separately: ownership, approval, payment, whether the quote changed.)*
   → *If My Home household specifically:* **"When you raise a request with the community, what actually happens step by step — who do you contact, how long does it usually take, and has it ever not worked the way you expected?"**
9. **"Did any item need to move somewhere?"**
10. **"When was the machine usable again?"**
    → **"Was there ever a point where the appliance/technician was gone and you had no idea when it would be resolved?"** *(Tests thread #20 — extended unavailability, a genuine national complaint pattern per `system_map.md` §7.1, not just a hypothesis.)*
11. **"What did this require from you?"** *(Active minutes, waiting time, days without the machine, actual expenses — kept separate, not conflated.)*

*— continue below only as time permits —*

12. **"Which moment was hardest, if any?"**
13. **"Was there a moment you postponed the next step?"**
14. **"Tell me about another repair that went differently."**
15. **"If a person you trusted had handled this incident, which actions could they take without checking with you?"**
    → **[HARDENED]** immediately follow with: **"Has there actually been a time you let someone else make that call for you — a family member, a friend, anyone? What happened?"** *(Grounds the hypothetical in real precedent — a "yes, once" answer is trustworthy signal; a pure hypothetical answer alone is weak, per the Mom Test audit in `research_plan.md` §9.)*
16. **"What would make you take the job back from them?"** Then: **"What record would reassure you it was finished?"** End with: **"What have I misunderstood or not asked?"**

**Segment-specific closers:**
- *Standalone Hyderabad household:* **[HARDENED]** **"Tell me about a time you tried to solve this some other way — hired help, asked a neighbour, anything."** *(Replaces the original "what would you want a building management service to do" — that was a pure hypothetical-wish question; this version asks about real attempted behavior instead.)*
- *IIITH campus:* **"Is this handled through campus facilities, or do you arrange it yourself even though you live on campus?"**
- *Renters only, any segment:* **"Would your landlord be open to a 10-minute conversation about how repairs get decided?"**

## B. Technician/service-centre guide — used for stakeholders #3, #4 (Eluru), #6 (My Home)

1. **"Walk me through the last household job from first contact to closure."**
2. **"How do you decide which job happens next?"**
   → *If My Home technician specifically:* **"How does a job reach you through the community — is there a fixed panel of vendors, a rotation, something else? What happens if you're not available?"** *(The single most valuable question in this whole guide — what an agent would need to replicate if My Home turns out to be the smoother case.)* Then: **"Are prices/rates for common jobs pre-agreed with the community, or negotiated fresh each time?"**
   → *If Eluru electrician specifically:* **"How do most of your customers find you — word of mouth, repeat customers, something else? Has that changed over the years?"**
3. **"Describe the last appointment that changed."**
4. **"What information is most often missing before a visit?"**
5. **"When can you quote accurately?"**
   → **"When a part was needed, how long did it actually take to arrive, and from where?"** *(The logistics-rail-completeness probe — tag every answer by location for the Eluru-vs-Hyderabad comparison, `narrowed_problem.md` Decision 1b.)*
6. **"Who is allowed to authorise work and payment?"**
7. **"What proves a job is complete, and what happens if the fault returns?"**
   → **"When a customer is due for a follow-up or repeat service, what actually reminds you to reach out — and how often does that step fail?"** *(Ask without assuming a formal AMC exists — "we don't really have that here" is itself a real finding for Eluru specifically, `system_map.md` §6.4.)*
8. **"What would make coordination by an assistant easier or harder?"** *(Ask last — distinguish hypothetical willingness from demonstrated acceptance.)*

**If a landlord/PG manager conversation materializes** (the snowball addition, `research_plan.md` §1): *"Describe the last disagreement about a repair cost." "What evidence did you need to approve it?" "Who admitted the technician?" "What was the last repair a tenant could approve themselves?"* Do not assume the tenant can transfer the owner's authority.

## After every single interview, before doing anything else

10-minute solo debrief: participant ID, role, date, facts, artifact IDs, exact quote + timestamp if recorded, your interpretation, a rival explanation, anything surprising, design implication, follow-up needed. Tag every claim **said / observed / artifact-supported / inferred / unknown** (`research/Fieldwork_Templates.md` §2). Log anything unexpected separately, in the running "what we weren't looking for" list (`research_plan.md` §6, point 4) — this is explicitly what the Evidence criterion rewards.
