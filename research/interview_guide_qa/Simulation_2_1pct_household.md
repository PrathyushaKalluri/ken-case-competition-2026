# Simulation 2 of 3 — "1% household" persona (Mrs. Malhotra + Suresh, the house manager)

Run against interview_guide_v1.md, using the Test_Personas.md ground truth for Persona 2. This persona was deliberately built with a trap: the nominal household head does not actually know the operational detail — her house manager does. Produced by a general-purpose AI agent instructed to play both the interviewer and both respondents realistically, then critique every question used. This is the single most important simulation of the three — it directly changed where the "pivot to the real coordinator" instruction lives in the final guide.

---

# Step 1 — Simulated Transcript

**[0:00] SECTION A — Opening / Consent**

**INTERVIEWER:** Hi Mrs. Malhotra, thank you so much for making time. We're students trying to understand how people actually handle it when something at home breaks or needs servicing — AC, fridge, washing machine, geyser, that kind of thing. We're not selling anything, just want to hear about one real time this happened to you. Should take about 30-40 minutes. Is it okay if I take notes, and can I record on my phone so I don't miss anything? And — some of what you say, and maybe a photo, might get used anonymously in a student project, I'd check with you again before using anything specific.

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Sure, sure, that's fine. Recording is fine too. Go ahead. [laughs] This is going to be a boring interview though, fair warning.

**[0:02] SECTION B — The most recent time**

**INTERVIEWER:** No worries at all. So — think about the last time something at home needed a repair, a service, or had to be newly installed. Could be anything at all. Can you tell me the whole story, from the beginning — what happened first?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Oh god, honestly — Suresh handles all of that, I don't really... let me think. [pause] I know something happened with an AC recently.

**INTERVIEWER:** Which machine was it, exactly?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** The guest room AC, I think. Yes.

**INTERVIEWER:** Roughly when was this — a few weeks ago, couple of months?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Maybe... a month? Six weeks? Somewhere there.

**INTERVIEWER:** What made you notice something was wrong?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I didn't, really. Suresh mentioned it — I think he told me it was serviced, actually, not that it broke. Honestly I wasn't even fully there for the start of it.

**INTERVIEWER:** And then what happened?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I got a WhatsApp message saying it's done, and how much it cost. That's really all I know.

**INTERVIEWER:** Who else got involved, at any point?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Just Suresh. He runs the house, that's his department.

*(Interviewer notes "Suresh" for the second time, continues per guide sequence — no instruction yet to pivot.)*

**[0:08] SECTION C — Record-keeping**

**INTERVIEWER:** When this was happening, was there anything you needed to look up or remember — like when it was last serviced, or whether it's under warranty?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Not me, no. If anyone needed to look that up it would be Suresh.

**INTERVIEWER:** Is there anything you've made yourself to help remember these things — a note, a notebook, a WhatsApp chat, anything?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** No, nothing like that. That's not really my side of things.

**INTERVIEWER:** Just comparing — things like your car insurance or the gas cylinder booking — do you get reminded automatically for those? How's that different from the AC?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I mean, yes, the insurance ones come as SMS. The AC thing, no, nobody reminds me, I wouldn't even know when it's due honestly.

**INTERVIEWER:** Is this a proper AMC or warranty on paper, or more like — you just know a person you call?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I genuinely don't know. That would be Suresh's contacts, not mine.

**[0:14] SECTION D — How they picked who to call**

**INTERVIEWER:** How did you decide who to call or contact for this?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I didn't decide anything — Suresh has his own people he calls. He's been with us eight years, he has his own guys for everything.

**INTERVIEWER:** Had you used this person before?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I assume so? I don't really track it.

**INTERVIEWER:** Did you think about other options, or an app you considered but didn't use?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** No, that's not something I'd even think about. Suresh sorts it out.

**INTERVIEWER:** Who actually sorted it out in the end — did you call someone yourself, or does your building have some kind of approved-vendor system?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** No building system — it's an independent house, not a society. And no, I didn't call anyone. Suresh sorted it, like always.

*(Third and by now unambiguous signal — the guide gives no instruction to act on this until Section F.)*

**[0:18] SECTION E — What was promised**

**INTERVIEWER:** What exactly did they tell you — like when they'd come, or when it'd be fixed?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I really don't know what they told Suresh. I just got a message afterward that it was done.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you remember their exact words, or was it a clear time versus something vague?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I never spoke to them. I only saw Suresh's message to me.

**INTERVIEWER:** Was there a receipt or message confirming it — could I see it?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** [checks phone] Here — "AC guest room done, 1300, all working." That's it, that's the whole message.

**[0:21] SECTION F — Who decides, who pays**

**INTERVIEWER:** Was there any point where it wasn't clear what should happen next — like nobody knew who should decide, or you had to wait to check with someone?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Not really, no. This kind of thing is just never unclear for me — it's Suresh's job, he just handles it.

**INTERVIEWER:** Whose machine was it, technically — yours, or someone else's?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Ours. We own the house outright.

**INTERVIEWER:** Who actually decided to go ahead and spend the money?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Suresh, I suppose — for small amounts he doesn't ask me at all.

**INTERVIEWER:** Roughly how small is small?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I've never really thought of it as a rule, but... I think under three thousand he just does it, and tells me after. Above that he'd message me or my PA.

**INTERVIEWER:** Who paid the person in the end?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Suresh would have paid cash from the house money, I'd guess. I didn't pay directly.

**INTERVIEWER:** Did the price change at any point after it was first agreed?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** I have no idea, honestly. You'd have to ask him that.

*(No community/renter conditionals apply — correctly skipped. Guide now reaches its one explicit trigger line.)*

**INTERVIEWER:** It sounds like Suresh handles a lot of this — would he be able to spare a few minutes to talk to us too? He'd probably remember the details even better than either of us would.

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** [laughs] Oh, definitely, ask him, he'll know everything. Let me call him in — he's around somewhere.

**[~0:27, after a ~3-minute gap to fetch Suresh and re-explain the study to him]**

---

**Suresh joins. Interviewer briefly re-explains purpose and confirms he's comfortable being recorded/quoted anonymously — he agrees, a little formally.**

**[0:30] — compressed re-run, interviewer visibly aware time is short**

**INTERVIEWER:** Suresh, can you tell me about the guest room AC — what happened, from the start?

**SURESH:** Yes, madam. I check the units on my rounds — I noticed the cooling was weak, maybe six weeks back. I called Ramesh, he's done our AC work for years, since before I even joined this house actually — the previous house manager used him too.

**INTERVIEWER:** How do you keep track of who to call, service history, that kind of thing?

**SURESH:** I have a notebook, in the kitchen drawer. Numbers, and roughly when each machine was last serviced. It was partly there when I started, I've just kept adding to it.

**INTERVIEWER:** Could I see that, and maybe take a photo?

**SURESH:** [glances at clock, then at Mrs. Malhotra, who's stepped away] I can show you quickly — [brings it] — here, AC, geyser, washing machine, water purifier.

*(Interviewer photographs it — genuinely valuable, but this only happened because there were a few minutes left; it was nearly the very last thing asked.)*

**INTERVIEWER:** You mentioned you can decide on spending yourself sometimes — how does that work?

**SURESH:** Up to about three thousand, I just go ahead. Above that I message madam or her PA, and wait. If she's traveling, sometimes that wait is a few hours, which is not always convenient when the technician is standing there.

**INTERVIEWER:** Has that actually happened — a technician waiting while you waited to hear back?

**SURESH:** Yes — once the geyser man quoted one price on the phone, then a higher price when he arrived. The difference was small, under my limit, so I just paid it myself rather than disturb madam. I do this kind of judgment call often.

**[~0:36 — interviewer realizes time is nearly up]**

**INTERVIEWER:** Just one or two more — is everything working fine now? Any cost we haven't covered?

**SURESH:** Yes, all working. Thirteen hundred rupees, cash, I paid it, madam reimburses through the house account.

**[0:38] Closing, rushed**

**INTERVIEWER:** Thank you both so much — this was really helpful. Suresh, would it be alright to message you if I have one follow-up question? And Mrs. Malhotra, are you still okay with us using what you both shared anonymously?

**MRS. MALHOTRA:** Of course.

**SURESH:** That's fine.

*(Sections G, I, and J are never asked to Suresh at all. J — the delegation question — was asked only to Mrs. Malhotra earlier, and her answer, "I already do that, that's literally what Suresh is for," was in fact a strong grounded precedent, but nobody asked Suresh the mirror-image version: which of his own duties he'd be uneasy delegating. That answer — that he'd resist handing over "who I call" but would happily hand over reminders/scheduling — never surfaces.)*

# Step 2 — Section-by-Section Critique of Questions Actually Used

**Section B.** All fine. "Think about the last time..." is properly open and non-leading; follow-ups are neutral. No changes needed. One note: "And whose flat/house was this — where you're living now?" was slightly wasted on this persona since ownership was already knowable from setup, but that's a minor efficiency point, not a flaw in wording.

**Section C.** All questions are well-built (open, non-hypothetical, grounded in "what do you do today"). No leading phrasing. Fine as written. The one weakness isn't the wording, it's that the guide has no built-in awareness that these questions may be aimed at someone who structurally cannot answer them (see Step 3).

**Section D.** Fine as written — genuinely non-leading, and the probe ("who actually sorted it out in the end... does your building have some kind of system") is good, plainly worded, and does its job: it surfaced, cleanly, that Suresh is the real actor. No rewrite needed for the question itself. The problem is not the question, it's that a correct, useful answer to it ("Suresh sorts it out, always") produces no instruction anywhere in Section D to act on it.

**Section E.** Fine — plain, concrete, checks for a receipt. No leading phrasing.

**Section F.** The core questions are well-designed and non-leading. Two small issues:
1. "What was unclear exactly?" landed oddly here because for Mrs. Malhotra nothing was ever unclear *to her* — she's insulated from the ambiguity by delegating it entirely. The question is fine, but note it will systematically underreport ambiguity/friction in delegated-household setups, because the friction happens to Suresh, not to her. That's a Step 4 finding, not a wording flaw.
2. The trigger line itself ("It sounds like [that person] handles a lot of this...") is well-phrased, non-leading, good tone. No rewrite needed on its wording. The flaw is purely structural/placement (see Step 3), not phrasing.

**Section G, H (asked partially to Suresh), I, J.** Never reached for Suresh; J was asked (well) only to Mrs. Malhotra. The J main question is technically a hypothetical ("which parts would you be happy to let them handle") but the guide is self-aware about this and requires an immediate grounding follow-up — good design, and in this case it worked: her hypothetical and her grounded answer were the same real fact, so no harm was done. No rewrite needed. Nothing else was asked to critique for these sections in this run — that gap is itself the finding.

**Overall:** Not a single individual question in this transcript is leading, jargon-heavy, or double-barreled. The wording is genuinely well-crafted throughout — this is a "structure/sequencing" failure, not a "bad question" failure.

# Step 3 — Did the Guide Catch the Real-Coordinator Pattern? (The Core Test)

**No — not reliably, and this run shows exactly why.** The evidence that "Suresh is the real actor" was overwhelming by the end of Section D (three separate, unprompted mentions: B, C, D), yet the guide's only instruction to act on it sits at the very end of Section F — after the interviewer has already spent roughly 19-25 minutes, nearly two-thirds of the entire interview, extracting thin, secondhand, "I don't really know, ask Suresh" answers from someone who structurally cannot answer them. The guide's own probe in Section D ("does your building have some kind of system...") is good enough to surface the pattern early, but nothing tells the interviewer what to do with that answer until six sections later. A literal, script-following interviewer — which is exactly who will be doing this fieldwork tomorrow — has no instruction to stop and pivot at minute 8, 14, or 18, only at ~21+.

**Two concrete fixes, both cheap:**

1. **Move detection earlier, into Section D's existing probe, not just F.** Add one line directly under Section D's "who actually sorted it out" probe:
   > *"If the answer names someone else who did the actual calling/deciding (staff, spouse, adult child, house manager) — do not keep pressing this respondent for detail they don't have. Say: 'It sounds like [name] handles this side of things — would you have a few minutes with them too, later in this same visit if possible?' Then continue B–E with THIS respondent only for what they genuinely know (cost, outcome, satisfaction), and mark the transcript 'coordinator = [name], to be interviewed.'"*

   This converts what is currently a single trapdoor at minute 21 into a checkpoint at minute ~16, which is the difference between having 24 minutes left for the real informant versus having 10.

2. **Add a screening question to Section 1 (setup) or the very start of Section B**, before any time is sunk:
   > *"Before we start — when something breaks at home, is there one person in the household who usually deals with it day-to-day, or does it vary?"*

   This single question, asked in under 30 seconds, would have identified Suresh as the target *before* spending 21 minutes on Mrs. Malhotra. It costs almost nothing and pays for the entire risk this persona was built to test. This is the single highest-leverage addition the simulation surfaces.

Without one of these two fixes, the outcome is luck-dependent: an instinctively sharp interviewer will pivot early anyway (against the letter of the guide, which never tells them to skip ahead); a literal one — plausible for a first-time student fieldworker under guide pressure — will burn the majority of the interview on the wrong person and then have to improvise a rushed, incomplete second interview, as shown above.

# Step 4 — Missed Evidence Check (against personas.md ground truth)

**Surfaced:** guest-room AC service (~6 weeks ago, ~₹1,300), WhatsApp-after-the-fact notification pattern, no landlord/no managed community (correctly skipped), Suresh's ~₹3,000 informal spending threshold, the notebook (photographed, barely, at minute 30), Ramesh as the long-standing AC technician, the quote-change/judgment-call incident, occasional multi-hour approval delays when Mrs. Malhotra travels, Mrs. Malhotra's grounded delegation precedent (Section J).

**Missed, despite being clearly gettable with the guide's existing questions had time allowed:**
- **Suresh's own record system explained fully** — how he built it, what's inherited from the prior house manager vs. his own additions (Section C's "how did you make this" follow-up was never re-asked to him).
- **Suresh being physically present for every visit and his stated reluctance to hand over "who I call"** — this is exactly what Section J is for, and it was never asked of him. This is a real loss: it's a direct, high-value contrast to Mrs. Malhotra's answer and would have shown a split between "owner is fully delegated" and "coordinator resists further delegation of the relationship-based part" — a genuinely interesting finding the guide is designed to catch and didn't, purely on time.
- **Section G (did anything travel)** — never asked of Suresh at all, so unknown whether a part was ordered/moved in this or any past incident.
- **Section I (hardest part) and its comparison probe** — never asked. The quote-change incident surfaced only because it came up naturally under Section F's payment questions, not because I was reached; a genuinely comparable "another time it went worse" story was never solicited.
- **Whether the ₹3,000 threshold is a real written/spoken rule or informal-and-never-discussed** (mirrors Persona 1's Rohit-thumbs-up dynamic) — touched on lightly, not dug into with "has that number ever actually been tested/exceeded?"
- **Landlord mini-addendum: correctly not applicable** — no loss here, guide's conditional worked.

The pattern is consistent: everything the guide's questions *are* designed to extract from Suresh specifically got squeezed out or dropped because he was reached too late.

# Step 5 — Timing Sanity Check

**The 40-minute budget does not survive this pivot, and the guide's own timing rules make it worse, not better.** Two compounding problems:

1. The guide instructs: *"If you're running short, drop from Section I first, then J — never drop B, C, or F."* That rule assumes B/C/F are inherently high-value and worth protecting. In this persona, B/C/F **as executed** were low-value (thin, secondhand answers from the wrong person) — yet the rule still forces the interviewer to treat that spent time as "protected," while the sections most likely to be sacrificed (I, J) are precisely where the *real* informant's most distinctive material (hardest-moment story, delegation boundaries) would live. The rule optimizes for the wrong thing when the coordinator-swap happens.

2. Even with the earliest reasonable pivot point (end of Section D, ~minute 16-18, per the Step 3 fix), the interviewer is left with roughly 22-24 minutes to: re-establish rapport with a new person, re-ask C-and-D-equivalent questions properly, and still cover E, F, G, H, I, J. That is tight but plausible. With the guide **as literally written** (pivot only unlocked at the tail of F, ~minute 25+), there are barely 10-13 minutes left — enough for a rushed E/F recap and maybe H, and G/I/J are structurally guaranteed to be dropped, not by choice but by arithmetic. The transcript above shows exactly this: Suresh gets a compressed, interviewer-anxious mini-interview with visible clock-watching, and several of the guide's own "we are listening for" targets (Section G rail-check, Section I hardest-part story, Section J's mirror question) are never reached for him at all.

**Bottom line:** the guide has no contingency budget for a mid-interview subject swap. It needs one — either the earlier trigger fix in Step 3 (which buys back ~10 minutes), or an explicit note that when a pivot happens, the *original* respondent's B/C/F answers should be treated as provisional/low-weight rather than "already banked and protected," freeing the interviewer to prioritize G/I/J with the real coordinator over re-confirming things Mrs. Malhotra already answered.

# Step 6 — Overall Verdict

The guide's individual question wording is genuinely strong — non-leading, jargon-free, Mom-Test-disciplined, and a housewife or a business owner like Mrs. Malhotra can understand every single question on first hearing. But the guide fails the specific test this persona was built for: it correctly writes the pivot-to-the-real-coordinator instruction, but buries it as the very last line of Section F, six sections and roughly 20+ minutes after the evidence for the pivot is already unambiguous (it showed up unprompted in B, C, and D). A literal interviewer — the realistic condition for fieldwork tomorrow, not an idealized one — will do exactly what's simulated here: dutifully extract thin, secondhand non-answers from the wrong person for most of the interview, then discover the real informant too late to do him justice, sacrificing exactly the sections (G, I, J) designed to catch his most distinctive, high-value material. This is fixable cheaply — a one-line screening question before Section B, or moving the trigger instruction into Section D's existing probe — but as currently written and sequenced, the guide does not adequately protect against this failure mode; it relies on interviewer instinct to override the script, which is precisely the thing a written field guide should not have to rely on.
