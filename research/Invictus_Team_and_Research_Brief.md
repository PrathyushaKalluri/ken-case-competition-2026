### Household interview questions, in order

1. Tell me about the most recent appliance repair or service you personally helped arrange. Approximately when was it?
2. What did you personally do, from noticing the need until the current outcome? What happened next?
3. Who owned the appliance? Who could approve the work? Who paid? Ask these separately.
4. How did you choose the provider? What alternatives did you consider?
5. What information did the provider ask for? Where did you find it?
6. What did the provider say would happen next? What actually happened?
7. After the first contact, what further calls, messages or visits occurred? Who initiated them?
8. Was there any point when the next action was unclear? If yes, walk me through that moment.
9. How was the price agreed? Did anything change after that agreement?
10. Did a part or the appliance need to move somewhere? If yes, who arranged it?
11. How did you decide that the job was finished? If it is not finished, what remains?
12. What, if anything, was difficult about arranging it? What worked well?
13. With your permission, can you show one relevant record or workaround that helps explain this incident?
14. If someone you trusted handled the arrangements, which decision would you still want them to bring back to you?

Use neutral follow-ups: "Can you give a specific example?"; "What happened then?"; "How do you know?"; "Is that remembered or shown in a record?". Get informed consent before interviewing, recording or collecting artifacts.

### Service-centre observation questions

1. Please walk me through how a household service request enters your system.
2. What information is needed before assigning a technician?
3. How is the technician chosen for a particular job?
4. How do you decide the visit time communicated to the customer?
5. Describe the most recent job whose assignment or visit time changed. What caused the change?
6. Who informs the customer and who owns the next action after a change?
7. When can a quote or required part be confirmed, and who approves changes?
8. What must happen before you mark a request complete?

With permission, observe a de-identified request through intake, assignment, contact, changes and closure. Record states, times, handoffs and missing information, not unrelated customer details. A short visit may not cover the entire lifecycle; distinguish directly observed events from the employee's reconstruction.
