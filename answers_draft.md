## Q4 — How your solution touches each rail
`One sentence each · no word cap displayed on the public page — check the live form`

**Payments & authorisation (Pine Labs — Grantex, P3P):** Grantex holds the household's standing authority — necessary because in **7 of 12 surveyed incidents the person who approved the cost was not the person who paid or who let the technician in** — and P3P releases money only against a number the household re-confirmed *after* the technician's scope changed mid-job.

**Logistics (Delhivery — Maps, MCP):** Weakest of the three on our evidence, and we tested it: **not one of 12 surveyed incidents involved a part or appliance being moved.** The parts leg exists on the supply side — one respondent was billed ₹200 "transportation" plus a marked-up part for a trip she never asked for [18:06] — but households do not experience it as logistics, which is precisely why it goes unpriced.

**Voice (Gnani):** The agent speaks to the technician in his own language, because every household in our sample reaches their technician by phone, and the one who went through a company helpline called that wait *"the most annoying thing ever"* [18:30].

**On Logistics — the position moved twice, and this is the honest landing.** The first draft called it thin. I then over-corrected: the interviews *do* contain parts friction (6128maggi's marked-up part and two sourcing trips [18:06–18:27], contrasted by her against Amazon/Blinkit same-day [17:47]; Sai Kakki's technician predicting the part from a photo of the model number, and the replacement proving poor quality only after use [15:45]; Sarala Aunty carrying a four-burner stove to the repair shop; Rk Sir noting car, laptop and phone all require household transport), and the team's own Miro technician journey map names the breakpoint outright — *"Source parts → wrong variant, stock shortage, extra trips."*

**But the survey disconfirms it from the demand side: 10 of 12 said no part moved, 2 said "not sure," none said yes.** The defensible reading is that the sourcing trip is real but invisible to the person financing it — a sharper claim than "logistics matters," and stated above as an interpretation rather than as corroboration. **Do not claim the survey supports a logistics rail. It does not.** This is also what makes choosing Payments for Q5 evidenced rather than assumed: we tested logistics and it failed.

**Honesty guardrail from `report-source.md` p.16:** Delhivery Maps/MCP exposes address, geocoding, route and distance tools — *it does not itself book a courier or guarantee a technician arrives*, and shipment creation sits in a separate portal whose access we have not verified. Do not imply otherwise.
