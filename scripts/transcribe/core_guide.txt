CORE INTERVIEWER SCRIPT — "Keeping the Machines Running" household/technician interviews
(Condensed reference: use this to recognize which speaker is the INTERVIEWER — their turns will
paraphrase or closely match these scripted questions. The RESPONDENT is whoever answers with
concrete personal stories, names, dates, amounts, and experiences. A THIRD_SPEAKER may appear
briefly — e.g. another household member, a technician being discussed/joining, a call-waiting
interruption, or a translator — usually a short handful of turns, not the main back-and-forth.)

OPENING / CONSENT (Section A):
"Hi, thank you for taking the time. We're students, and we're trying to understand how people in
India actually handle it when something in the house breaks down or needs a repair or a service —
things like the AC, the fridge, the washing machine, the geyser, the water purifier. We're not
selling anything. We just want to hear about one real time this happened to you..."
"Is it okay if I take notes while we talk? And would you be okay if I also just record the
conversation on my phone?"
"When something breaks down at home, is there one particular person who usually ends up dealing
with it, or does it really vary?"

SECTION B — Most recent repair (main): "Think about the last time something at home needed a
repair, a service, or had to be newly installed... Can you tell me the whole story, from the very
beginning — what happened first?"
Follow-ups: Roughly when was this? Which machine was it, exactly? Whose flat/house was this?
Probes: What made you notice something was wrong? What did you do right after? And then what
happened? Who else got involved?

SECTION C — Record-keeping / small unreported things: "When this was happening, was there anything
you needed to look up or remember — like when it was last serviced, whether it's still under
warranty, or who you called last time?" ... "Is there anything you've made yourself, to help you
remember these things?" ... "Just thinking about other things you have to renew or remember for the
house — like insurance or a gas cylinder — do you get reminded about those automatically?" ...
"Is there a paper contract where you pay once a year for regular servicing — an AMC — or do you
just know a person you always call?" ... "Has there ever been something small at home that needed
fixing that you just never got around to calling anyone about?"

SECTION D — Who they called, who decides: "How did you decide who to call or contact for this?"
"Had you used this person or company before? Did you think about other options?" "Does your
building or society have any kind of official system for this?" "Thinking just about this one
time — did you actually end up using that, or did you do something else?"

SECTION E — What was promised: "What exactly did they tell you — like when they'd come, or when it
would be fixed?" "Did they give you a clear time, or something vague like soon?" "Did what they said
would happen actually happen?"

SECTION F — Who decides, who pays: "Was there any point where it wasn't clear what should happen
next?" "Whose machine was it, technically?" "Who actually decided to go ahead and spend the money?"
"Who paid the person in the end?" "Did the price change at any point after you first agreed to it?"
(If renter) "Did you need to check with anyone before you could go ahead — a landlord, family
elder, or building office? How long did it take to hear back? Once you asked to be paid back or
adjusted, how long did that take?"

SECTION G — Did anything travel: "Did any part, or the whole machine, need to be picked up, sent
somewhere, or delivered?" "Who arranged that? How long did that part take?" "Has that ever been
different, another time?"

SECTION H — Fixed now / cost: "Is it fully sorted now, or is anything still pending?" "How do you
know it's actually fixed?" "From the moment you first noticed the problem to the moment it was
actually working again — how long did that take?" "How much of your own time did this take?" "Did
this cost you any money out of pocket? Roughly how much?"

SECTION I — Hardest part: "Looking back at the whole thing, was there one part that was the most
difficult or the most annoying?" "What exactly happened at that point? What did you end up doing?"

SECTION J — Handing it over: "If someone you really trusted offered to fully handle something like
this for you next time, which parts would you be happy to let them do without checking with you
first?" (grounding follow-up, always asked) "Has there actually been a time in the past where you
did let someone else make one of these calls for you?"

CLOSING: "What would make you feel sure the job was actually finished properly?" "Is there anything
about all this that I should have asked but didn't?" "Thank you so much, this was really useful..."

LANDLORD MINI-ADDENDUM (if applicable): "Tell me about the last time a tenant asked you to fix or
replace something." "How did you decide whether to approve it, and how much it should cost?" "Who
let the technician into the property?" "Is there anything a tenant can just go ahead and fix
themselves, without asking you first?"

NOTE: Real calls may follow this loosely, skip sections, reorder, or add extra ad-hoc questions
suited to the respondent (e.g. if the respondent is a technician/service person rather than a
householder, questions will pivot toward their side of the job — how they get called, how they
quote, how they get paid, what goes wrong). Use conversational structure (who asks vs. who narrates
personal experience) as the primary signal, not exact word match.
