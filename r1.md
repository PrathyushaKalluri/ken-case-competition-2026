## Addendum 2 — Independent secondary research on task-substitution & service-tracking

Full detail: `system_map.md` §6, `notes.md` §6b.

**Q6 (customer asset)**: the strongest candidate to emerge from this research pass. India's vehicle-reminder ecosystem (government SMS alerts, the mParivahan app, third-party WhatsApp reminders) works because every party — insurer, PUC centre, transport department — can hook into one shared anchor: the RTO registration number. Appliances have no equivalent shared identifier across brands. That suggests the customer asset your agent asks for may not be a data export (order history, contacts) but the *role itself* — becoming the one place that holds a stable identifier per appliance, across brands, that nothing else currently provides. Flagged `[HYPOTHESIS]` — see `plan.md`'s new probe #2 before writing this into Q6.

**Q9 (which Indian company should've built this)**: sharpens the framing from "which appliance/marketplace company" toward "why hasn't the *mechanism* that already works for vehicles been extended to appliances." A candidate answer: no single company owns an equivalent to RTO registration for appliances, so no natural anchor exists for any one player to build from — which itself could be your answer to "why they haven't," independent of which specific company you name.

**Q2 (the insight, if independently corroborated in real interviews — not before)**: the finding that Indian AMC/service vendors themselves track renewals in Excel with reminder calls "from memory," losing 20-30% of renewals to forgetting, complicates the household-is-the-weak-link framing. If your own supply-side interviews confirm this, the sharper insight may be that **both sides of the relationship are relying on informal memory, not just the household** — which changes what "the agent fixes" needs to mean (it can't just be a better reminder for the customer if the vendor's own pipeline is equally leaky).
