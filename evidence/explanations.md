# Evidence — explanation of the photographs

Team TripleIT · The Ken Case Competition 2026 · Opening "Keeping the machines running"

These are photographs of the systems households have built by hand to keep track of their machines and the people who service them. Each one is explained below: what it is, and what the household uses it for.

---

## 1. Gas cylinder — `workarounds/Gas cylinder.jpeg`

An Indane LPG cylinder in use, connected to the regulator and hose.

**"15/7/26" is written by hand on the body of the cylinder.**

This is the date the cylinder was taken from the company. The household writes it on the cylinder itself so they know how long that cylinder has been running. By comparing the date written on the cylinder against the date it runs out, they learn how many days or months one cylinder lasts for their household, and use that to judge when to book the next one and when to keep a spare ready.

The cylinder already carries printed information from the company — an emergency number, net and gross weight, and a batch number and "change before" date printed on the hose. None of that tells the household how long their own cylinder will last. That is why the date is added by hand.

---

## 2. Water purifier — `workarounds/Padmaja RO service date.jpeg`

A Havells Gracia Alkaline RO water purifier.

**"NEXT 15/M/26" is written in marker directly on the machine.**

This is the date the next filter service is due. The household writes it on the machine so they know when the filter has to be changed next, because nothing reminds them otherwise.

Taped below it is a separate card printed with four phone numbers for a local service agency, Kumari Agencies, Visakhapatnam.

The factory sticker underneath already carries a QR code, the model code, the serial number and two manufacturer toll-free numbers. The household has written over that sticker with the service date, and taped the local agency's numbers next to it. Those are the numbers they actually call.

## 3. Sarala Aunty's diary — `workarounds/sarala aunty notes 1.jpeg`, `Sarala aunty notes 2.jpeg`, `Sarala aunt notes 4.jpeg`

Handwritten pages from a personal diary, kept in Telugu and English, recording household repair and service work.

The household uses these pages to remember and to follow up on:

- **when the last repair was done** — dates are written against each job
- **how much was paid** — the total agreed, the advance paid, and the balance still outstanding
- **the phone numbers of the workers** — recorded next to the job they did
- **what the service was for** — the specific work, such as gate work, painting, electrical points and tubelight fitting
- **what the next step on that service is** — what is still pending and what has to be arranged next

The pages show job amounts, an advance paid against a total with a running balance, dates, and the contact numbers of the tradespeople written alongside the work they carried out.

*Note: `Sarala aunty notes 2.jpeg` and `sarala aunty notes 3.jpeg` are the same photograph. Submit one of them.*

## 4. Team working notes — `Workflows/` folder

Photographs from the team's own brainstorming and mapping sessions, included as our working notes.

- **`Brainstorming 1.jpeg` – `Brainstorming 7.jpeg`** — whiteboard sessions covering the stakeholder and persona map, the machine and errand list, the workflow from breakdown to resolution, coordination and turnaround time, data sources, and the deliverables for this submission.
- **`Workflow 1.jpeg`** — problem discovery map: who is involved, what breaks, what people want, and where the process fails.
- **`Workflow 2.jpeg`** — the household's user journey map, from machine breakdown through finding help, explaining and booking, diagnosis and approval, repair and follow-up, to verification, with the pain points marked at each stage.
- **`Workflow 3.jpeg`** — the technician's user journey map, from receiving the enquiry through prioritising, travelling, inspecting, explaining the diagnosis and cost, sourcing the part, fixing, and getting paid, with the pain points marked at each stage.
- **`Journey Map.jpeg`** — the technician journey redrawn with the actions, thoughts, emotions, pain points and touchpoints laid out stage by stage.
