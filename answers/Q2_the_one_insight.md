# Q2 — The one customer insight

**Status: LOCKED, exactly 60 words. Judges read this one first.**

> **The question, verbatim:** One insight about this problem that is missing from our description of the openings, that you learned from people who live this pain… An insight is something non-obvious you now understand about the person you are building for — how they think about this problem, what they are really protecting, why they do something that looks irrational from the outside. It earns its place when it changes your solution: a step in your loop that exists because of it, or works differently because of it. Give us the insight, and show us where it landed in what you designed.

---

## THE ANSWER (60 words)

> Nothing in a repair can be checked. The price is a guess, the part may be fake, "job done" proves nothing. So people judge the person, not the work. A woman let a carpenter approve spending on her house in another city after watching him fix one door. Our agent earns control the same way, one job at a time.

---

## The insight in plain words

A household cannot verify anything about a repair.

- They are never given a **price**, only a range.
- They cannot tell whether the **part** that went in is genuine.
- **"Job done"** — from a technician, an app or a ticket — tells them nothing about whether it will hold.

Because checking the *work* is impossible, they stopped trying. They check the **person** instead.

And they do that in one specific way: **they watch someone do a small job, and if it goes well, they give that person more power.** Not more work — more *authority*. That is the non-obvious part. Trust here is not a rating, a brand, or a certificate. It is earned by being watched, and it is paid out in permission.

**Why it looks irrational from outside:** a woman handed a carpenter the right to approve spending on a property in a different city — a property she rarely visits — on the strength of one door repair. On paper that is reckless. In a market where nothing can be verified, it is the only rational thing available: she bought the one thing that outlasts a warranty, a quote or a ticket, which is a person who showed up and behaved well while she was watching.

## Where it landed in the design

**This is the part the question specifically asks for, and it is why the last sentence exists.**

Sarala's trust did not arrive all at once. Read the sequence: one small door repair → *"since then I have been able to trust him and **approve his small works**"* → eventually two properties in two cities she does not live in. **Trust escalated in steps, and every step was paid for by a job she had seen go well.**

So the agent cannot ask for authority up front. Every product in this space does — install the app, set a spend cap, grant permissions, then discover after a disaster that the trust was never earned. Our loop inverts that:

1. The agent starts on **one small job**, low stakes, fully visible.
2. It **shows the household what happened** — the quote, the part, the technician, the fix.
3. Only then does it get **more room to act without asking**.
4. Each later expansion is bought with another completed, seen job.

**The mandate grows with observed performance instead of being granted at signup.** That is "watching" made operational, and it is a step in the loop that exists only because of this insight. Delete the insight and the agent reverts to asking for a spend cap on day one, like everyone else.

**It also sets up the rail.** The gap in Pine Labs' P3P/Grantex is not a spend cap — that already exists. It is that **no payment rail today lets delegated authority grow from evidence.** Authority is granted, not earned, and modifying it requires the human's UPI PIN. That is a real, verified, defensible Q5.

## What was rejected, and why

**"The first repair visit is an information trip disguised as a technician visit."** Tested and dropped. Two reasons. It contains no household psychology — nothing is being protected, nothing looks irrational, and no household said it. It is an operations insight about technician travel, which belongs in Q3/Q5, not the field asking about the person you are building for. And its foundation fails: **survey Q13 returned 10 "No", 2 "Not sure", 0 "Yes"** on whether any part or appliance had to be moved, and across all seven interviews there is exactly **one** genuine diagnose-then-return case. That case (6128maggi **[17:00]**) complains about *unauthorised spend*, not travel. The two-trip premise comes from the mentor's 50-km village arithmetic **[00:29, 01:03]**, imported onto a Tier-2 urban persona.

**Note:** survey Q12 measures *phone follow-ups*, not technician visits. Do not cite its 6/12 as second-trip evidence — that misreads our own instrument.

**"Paying commissions he never asked for."** Cut. N=1 (Sarala only) and the translation stutters. The insight stands on four independent sources without it.

**Gender as the segmentation variable.** Rejected. 11-of-19 women is a recruitment artefact. The mentor floats it at **[32:24]** then immediately withdraws it at **[32:50]**: *"this is a discovery problem… I can't say something out loud."*
